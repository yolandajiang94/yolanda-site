In 2017, after graduating from university, I moved from Wuhan to Shenzhen.

During university, I had heard that Shenzhen was a good place to start a career in foreign trade, and I saw some older students from my school move to Guangdong for work after graduation.

There was also a simpler reason I was drawn to Shenzhen. I grew up in Hubei, where winters are cold and damp. When I heard about Shenzhen's warm winters, sunshine, and blue skies, the city naturally became part of my imagination of a better life.

So after graduation, I came to Shenzhen with two close friends by train.

### **My First Step Into Industrial B2B Sales**

Before graduation, I found my first job through campus recruitment. It was an LED lighting company serving industrial markets, and I joined as a foreign trade salesperson after receiving my graduation certificate in June 2017.

More than ten new graduates joined around the same time, and we went through intensive training. We practiced presentations almost every day and were constantly pushed to improve our communication skills.

A few months later, in September 2017, the company arranged for another colleague and me to spend six months in the United States to explore potential business opportunities. An experienced colleague went with us to provide support with practical matters, but business development still depended largely on ourselves.

We were new graduates with limited sales experience. We had the courage to try, but we did not yet know how to develop business independently in an overseas market or turn conversations into real opportunities.

The six months in the United States did not bring the business progress we had hoped for. For a long time, I blamed myself and felt that I had failed to make good use of the opportunity.

Looking back, it was the first time I clearly saw the gap between enthusiasm and sales capability.

### **Moving Into Key Account Support**

After returning to China, the company was expanding its business with brand customers, and a newly promoted key account manager needed an assistant to support customer projects and daily coordination. I accepted the role.

For the next two years, my work focused more on following up on customer projects, communicating with clients, coordinating internal teams, tracking details and progress, and supporting longer sales cycles.

This role became an important part of my early career. Although I had originally joined the company as a foreign trade salesperson focused on customer development, I gradually gained more experience in customer communication, project follow-up, and cross-functional coordination.

### **Leaving My First Job**

The working environment during that period was demanding. The pace was fast, overtime was common, and I began questioning whether this was the working style I wanted in the long term.

In April 2020, after nearly three years with the company, I decided to leave and explore another direction.

At the time, I thought I was moving away from international B2B sales. But this first job had already given me real experience in industrial B2B and laid an important foundation for my later career.
