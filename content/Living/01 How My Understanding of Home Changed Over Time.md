# **How My Understanding of Home Changed Over Time**

## **From Survival Space to Living Space**

After graduating in 2017, I moved to Shenzhen.

During my first year of work, I lived in the company dormitory near the industrial park.

At that stage, as a young person just starting my career, my living environment was not something I paid much attention to. Work, personal growth, and gaining experience were much more important.

In August 2018, my department moved to an office in Futian, the central area of Shenzhen, and I started renting a place on my own.

At that time, I had very few requirements for where I lived.

I rented a typical apartment in an urban village.

It was a very simple single room:

one room, one bathroom, and a small balcony.

There was no real window in the room. The only natural light came from the area connecting the kitchen and balcony. During the daytime, I often needed to turn on the lights.

The kitchen was more like a narrow passage with a cooking area.

The room was completely empty.

I bought a bed online and assembled it piece by piece by myself.

Looking back now, it was not a comfortable living environment.

But at that time, I did not feel disappointed.

---

## **Making the Best of What I Had**

For many years, I never seriously considered improving my living environment.

Not because I did not need it, but because at that stage, I believed it was the most suitable choice for me.

I enjoyed living alone and never considered sharing an apartment.

In Shenzhen, finding a place as a single person already limits many options.

Although urban village apartments were simple, they provided me with my own private space and matched my financial situation and lifestyle at that time.

I always believed:

**As long as I kept my room clean and organized, I could still live a good life.**

So during those years, I spent a lot of time taking care of my small space.

Every weekend, I cleaned my room, washed clothes, and tried to make the best use of what I had.

I also enjoyed living independently.

On weekends, I would go to the local market, buy fresh vegetables and meat, and prepare a proper meal for myself.

At that time, I did not feel that my life was lacking.

---

## **Gradually Accepting Less Than I Needed**

Over the following years, I moved several times.

In March 2021, I moved to another apartment in the same urban village.

It was still a single room with basic conditions, but with slightly better lighting.

In March 2023, I moved into a one-bedroom apartment in the same building.

The space became larger, but the overall living environment did not fundamentally change.

Looking back now, I realize something:

Sometimes, people do not stop needing a better life.

They simply learn to ignore those needs when they have limited choices.

When an environment becomes our daily reality, it is easy to tell ourselves:

“This is good enough.”

Because there were no better options at that time, I adapted and adjusted myself to the situation.

I told myself:

A smaller room is fine.

Less natural light is fine.

As long as it is clean and organized, it is enough.

This was not about deceiving myself.

It was simply the way I dealt with reality at that stage of life.

---

## **A Different Experience of Living**

In March 2025, I finally moved away from the urban village where I had lived for many years.

I increased my rental budget and moved into an apartment building.

This time, my daily experience changed significantly.

The apartment itself was brighter and cleaner.

More importantly, I no longer needed to spend so much time compensating for the limitations of the space.

In the past, I had to constantly clean and organize my room to make a basic environment feel comfortable.

Now, the space itself supported my life.

For the first time, I realized:

**A better living environment can reduce many invisible burdens.**

---

## **Finding a Place That Feels Like Home**

<img src="/images/balcony-view.jpg" width="800">

In April 2026, I moved into a community that I had been looking forward to for a long time.

Looking back, this was the first time after nearly ten years in Shenzhen that I truly felt the comfort and openness that a home could bring.

This community is quite special in a city like Shenzhen.

It consists entirely of low-rise buildings, without high-rise residential towers, with a relatively low density and plenty of greenery.

In a city filled with high-rise buildings, this kind of living environment is becoming increasingly rare.

I do not particularly envy living in the most central areas of the city.

I also do not seek luxurious decoration or expensive furniture.

What I truly value is actually very simple:

sunlight, open space, greenery, and a place where I can truly relax after returning home.

And this place happens to provide exactly what I have been looking for.

I moved into a three-bedroom apartment.

The living room connects to two open balconies facing different directions, creating an open and breathable space where I can feel natural sunlight and fresh air.

Every room, including the kitchen and bathroom, has windows.

From different parts of the apartment, I can see natural views outside.

When my friends saw photos of the place, some of them even thought I was living in a villa.

But what makes me happy is not the size of the apartment itself.

It is the feeling of living in this environment every day.

In the morning, sunlight naturally enters the room.

After work, when I walk back into the community and see the greenery and open space around me, I gradually feel relaxed.

The staff at the entrance greet me when I come home.

The gate even says:

“Welcome home.”

After dinner, I often take a walk around the community.

I walk through the small commercial street and look at the vegetables and fruits in the supermarket.

Sometimes, discounted vegetables in the evening only cost a few yuan.

These small and ordinary moments give me a very simple but genuine sense of happiness.

At that moment, I started wondering:

**How much do we really need to earn in order to have the life we want?**

---

## **Rethinking What We Are Working For**

For many years, I believed:

If I worked hard, endured difficulties, and kept moving forward, life would eventually become better.

There is nothing wrong with this belief.

When we are young, accepting less comfortable conditions in order to build our careers and gain experience is part of many people's journeys.

The years I spent living in urban villages also gave me many valuable things.

I learned how to live independently and how to take care of myself.

Those experiences are not something I need to reject.

They shaped who I am today.

But now, at 32, I have started to rethink what effort really means.

Working hard should not only be about creating a better future someday.

Because every day we are living now is also a real part of life.

I used to think I was someone who could endure anything.

But later I realized:

When people have the ability to choose, everyone hopes to have a better life.

That does not mean becoming less ambitious.

It means learning to respect our own feelings.

---

## **What Home Means to Me Now**

In the past, I thought:

Home was simply a place to sleep and rest.

Now, I see home differently.

Home is the space where we spend time with ourselves every day.

It influences our emotions, our physical state, and the way we experience life.

I am not denying the places where I lived before.

Those homes witnessed my early years in Shenzhen and accompanied me through the beginning of my career.

But now, when I have more choices, I finally allow myself to pursue a living environment that truly fits me.

A good living environment is not a luxury.

It is support.

It helps me stay calm, gives me more energy to face work, and reminds me:

**After years of working hard, we can slowly build a life that feels like our own.**
