# **Expanding My Life Beyond Familiar Places**

## **From Staying in One Place to Exploring New Possibilities**

For many years, my life in Shenzhen followed a very stable pattern.

I worked, lived, and spent most of my time within a familiar area. The route between my home and workplace became part of my daily routine. I knew the streets, the restaurants, the transportation, and the surroundings very well.

Looking back, this stability gave me a sense of security.

But at the same time, I gradually became less aware of how much my life had become centered around one familiar place.

Not because I disliked exploring.

Simply because life was always filled with things that seemed more urgent:

work responsibilities, financial considerations, daily routines, and the effort of building a career in a competitive city.

When we are focused on adapting to life, we sometimes forget to ask whether the life we have built is still the life we want.

---

## **A Year When Everything Started to Change**

In 2026, several important parts of my life changed within a short period of time.

I moved away from the place where I had lived for many years.

I ended a relationship.

I left the company where I had worked for five years.

For a long time, many parts of my life had remained unchanged because changing them required energy, courage, and uncertainty.

But when several things changed at the same time, I suddenly realized:

Maybe my life was not as fixed as I had imagined.

A familiar routine can feel comfortable, but it can also quietly limit our imagination.

Sometimes, we do not stay because something is the best choice.

We stay because it is the choice we already know.

---

## **Discovering a Different Relationship With a City**

After temporarily leaving Shenzhen and making Huizhou my temporary base, I started seeing the surrounding cities from a different perspective.

One weekend, I visited Huicheng District, the urban center of Huizhou, with my friend.

We originally planned to spend only the weekend there. After my friend returned on Sunday, I decided to stay one more day and explore the city at my own pace.

I walked around different areas, searched for restaurants, took buses and taxis, and visited places that I had always wanted to see but never actually made time for.

What surprised me was not any specific attraction.

It was the feeling of having time and space to simply experience a place.

I saw rivers flowing through the city.

I walked along streets with local restaurants that had existed for many years.

I noticed that a city could offer more than convenience and work opportunities.

It could also provide a rhythm of life.

---

## **Beyond the Boundaries I Had Created**

For many years, Shenzhen represented almost everything I needed:

career opportunities, a familiar environment, and a sense of belonging.

Because Shenzhen has played such an important role in my life, I naturally assumed that my future would continue to be built around it.

But after spending more time in nearby cities, I started to see the Pearl River Delta differently.

Shenzhen, Dongguan, and Huizhou are not completely separate worlds.

They are connected by geography, transportation, and people's daily lives.

Instead of thinking:

“Where should I stay forever?”

I started thinking:

“How can I build a life with more flexibility?”

A place does not have to be a permanent answer.

It can simply be a choice for a certain stage of life.

---

## **Learning to Appreciate Movement**

For a long time, I thought stability meant staying in one place.

Now I am beginning to understand that stability can also come from having the ability to adapt.

Moving to a new area, changing jobs, and exploring unfamiliar places can feel uncomfortable.

But they also remind us that we are capable of creating a new routine.

The past several months have been a period of saying goodbye:

to an old home,

to an old relationship,

to an old career stage.

But they have also been a period of discovering new possibilities.

---

## **Looking Forward**

I do not know exactly where I will eventually settle down.

Maybe it will be Shenzhen.

Maybe another city nearby.

Maybe somewhere I have not considered yet.

But I no longer feel the same need to find one permanent answer immediately.

What matters more is knowing that I have the ability to choose, adjust, and explore.

After years of building a life around familiarity, I am learning that a meaningful life is not only about finding a place to stay.

It is also about keeping the freedom to move forward.
