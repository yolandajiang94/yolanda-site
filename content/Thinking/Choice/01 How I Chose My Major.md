
# How I Chose My Major and Started My Career Journey

In the summer of 2013, I made one of the first important decisions in my life: choosing my university major.

At that time, I was a student from a rural area in China, with limited access to information and guidance about university choices and future careers.

I did not have many resources or people around me who could provide guidance on university choices or career planning. I did not own a computer, and I rarely had access to the internet. Most of the information I could get came from occasional visits to internet cafes or the computer rooms at school.

Compared with students who had access to more information, professional advice, or family guidance, my understanding of different universities, majors, and future career paths was quite limited.

My score was only two points below the admission cutoff line for the first undergraduate admission category in China’s college entrance system.

It was not a poor result. However, missing that cutoff meant I needed to make my choice among universities in another undergraduate admission category, and carefully consider how to make the best use of my score.

At that time, Chinese universities were commonly divided into different admission categories based on entrance exam scores. The first category generally had higher admission requirements, while the second category included other undergraduate universities with relatively lower score requirements.

For me, the challenge was not simply choosing a university.

It was choosing a direction for my future.

---

## **Choosing a University and Major**

At first, I considered studying outside Hubei Province.

I wanted to experience a different environment and believed leaving my hometown could be a new beginning.

However, when it was time to make the final decision, I chose Wuhan, a city I was more familiar with.

Then came the next question:

How could I make the best use of my score?

One of the majors I considered was fashion design at Wuhan Textile University.

It was one of the university’s strongest programs, and at that time I thought choosing a well-known major might be a smart decision.

However, my aunt told me that working in the fashion industry could be physically and mentally demanding.

After thinking about it, I realized that I was not truly interested in fashion itself.

I was attracted more by the reputation of the major than by the actual work behind it.

So I continued exploring other options.

Eventually, I noticed Hubei University of Economics.

It was one of the more competitive undergraduate universities in the province. Accounting was considered one of its strongest majors, but based on my score, it might have been difficult for me to enter.

Another option was International Economics and Trade.

This major immediately attracted my attention.

Not because I fully understood international trade at that time.

But because of the image I associated with it.

---

## **The Career I Imagined**

I was fascinated by the idea of becoming an international business professional.

In my imagination, studying International Economics and Trade meant that one day I might:

· travel overseas for business trips;

· communicate confidently with clients from different countries;

· work in an international environment;

· become a professional business person representing a company globally.

I had always admired that kind of professional image.

I imagined myself wearing professional clothes, communicating with people from different cultures, and building a career connected with the wider world.

Even years later, when choosing clothes, I was still attracted to suits and structured outfits because they represented the kind of professional identity I once imagined.

Another reason was the perceived career potential.

I heard that foreign trade salespeople could earn commissions through international business, which made me believe this career path might provide more possibilities for personal growth.

With these expectations, I made my decision.

I chose International Economics and Trade.

---

## **From Imagination to Reality**

Looking back today, I realize that my choice was based on a mixture of reality and imagination.

At that time, I did not fully understand what international trade work actually involved.

I had never experienced the daily reality of foreign trade:

· writing emails repeatedly;

· preparing quotations;

· following up with customers;

· coordinating with factories;

· managing countless details behind every project.

The “international business professional” I imagined and the real-life foreign trade salesperson were very different.

But I do not regret that decision.

Because at that moment, I made the best choice I could with the information and understanding I had.

A person's choices are always limited by the world they can see at that stage of life.

The important thing is not whether an early choice was perfect.

The important thing is whether we can continue learning, adjusting, and understanding ourselves better along the way.

My journey from studying International Economics and Trade to becoming an Industrial B2B Sales professional was not a straight line.

It was a process of gradually replacing imagination with experience, and expectation with understanding.
