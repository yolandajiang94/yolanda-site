# **A Step-by-Step Approach to Troubleshooting a Low-Current Testing Issue**

## **Background**

A research customer contacted us after observing abnormal results during low-current battery testing.

The customer initially suspected that two testing units might have hardware issues because other units of the same model were performing normally.

At the beginning of the discussion, the available information was limited. The customer described the abnormal behavior, but detailed test data was not immediately available.

My role was to help organize the information, communicate with the customer, and work with internal teams to gradually identify the possible cause.

---

## **Step 1: Clarifying the Testing Conditions**

Before involving deeper technical analysis, I first reviewed the customer's testing conditions together with the product specifications.

The initial testing condition was close to the lower limit of the equipment capability.

Instead of immediately treating the issue as a hardware failure, I suggested that the customer first perform testing within the specified operating range.

This step helped us separate two possibilities:

· whether the issue was caused by testing conditions outside the equipment capability;

· or whether there was a real performance issue under normal operating conditions.

---

## **Step 2: Collecting More Reliable Data**

After the customer performed additional tests within the recommended range, the abnormal results still appeared.

At this stage, the discussion moved from a possible usage condition issue to a more specific technical investigation.

I worked with the customer to collect additional test information and suggested testing at different current levels.

By comparing the results under different conditions, we were able to narrow down the range where the abnormal behavior occurred.

This provided a clearer direction for further analysis.

---

## **Step 3: Working with Internal Teams on Further Evaluation**

Based on the collected information, I coordinated with our R&D and service teams for further evaluation.

The analysis suggested that the issue might be related to low-current accuracy and calibration.

Further technical verification and service support were discussed as possible next steps.

Throughout the process, my responsibility was not to make technical conclusions myself, but to make sure that:

· customer information was accurately communicated internally;

· technical teams had the necessary data for analysis;

· the customer understood the progress and next steps.

---

## **Outcome**

Through several rounds of communication and verification, the issue was gradually narrowed from a general equipment concern to a more specific technical area related to low-current performance.

The troubleshooting process established a clearer direction for further service evaluation and helped both the customer and internal teams better understand the situation.

---

## **Reflection**

Complex industrial problems are rarely solved through one single answer.

They often require a structured process:

understand the situation → verify assumptions → collect evidence → narrow down possibilities → determine the next action.

For a sales engineer, creating this process and connecting different parties is often as important as finding the final technical solution.
