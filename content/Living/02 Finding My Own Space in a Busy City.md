I have always been someone who likes to organize things.

In the past, this habit mostly focused on the small, practical details of everyday life.

Over the past year, however, this long-standing habit began to extend to more complex and long-term questions.

Turning something messy into something structured has always given me a strong sense of satisfaction.

And much of that process this year took place in cafés.

Outside of work, I had much less of a social life than usual. Whenever I had time on weekends, I took my laptop to a café. Sometimes I sorted things out; other times, I simply sat there and let my mind wander.

Looking back, I regularly visited six or seven cafés across several districts of Shenzhen, and later in Huizhou and Dongguan.

## **Starting with a Small Café**

From around October last year, I started going regularly to a small café near Baishilong in Longhua District.

Located in an urban village, it was smaller than the spacious cafés I later came to prefer.

But the owner had a great sense of style. One side of the café was covered by a large glass wall, and it was a comfortable place to sit.

The market where I usually bought groceries was nearby, so after the café, I would sometimes buy some vegetables and go home to cook. The café was simply part of my daily routine then.

At the time, I was still doing what I had always done: going through years of personal notes and records, adding, deleting, and reorganizing them.

As I began using AI more frequently, my attention gradually shifted to my old work spreadsheets and overseas sales development system, as well as questions in my life that I had been struggling to make sense of.

## **Making Space to Sort Things Out**


<img src="/images/luohu-cafe-on-a-rainy-day.jpg" alt="A café in Luohu on a rainy day" width="800">



Around February and March this year, I started going regularly to a café in Luohu District.

It was not close to home, but I liked the environment enough to keep going back.

Compared with the small café in Baishilong, it felt much more open. It had almost everything I later realized I liked in a café: plenty of space, an outdoor area, good natural light and views, and a location away from crowded shopping malls.

I also loved its high ceiling and slightly industrial-style interior. The music was good too, and never too loud.

By then, many things I had been trying to sort through were much clearer, and nothing felt especially urgent to solve.

I would often spend hours there, slowly sorting through things in my life, work, and mind. I still had things to figure out, but I could take my time, and the process itself felt comfortable.

Once, a sudden rainstorm hit. I noticed a man sitting in an outdoor seat with a great view. The wind and rain were pouring down, but he stayed there, completely relaxed.

## **Under the Flame Trees**


<img src="/images/cafe-under-a-flame-tree.jpg" alt="A café under the flame trees" width="800">


Later, I moved to Bantian, where I lived in a residential community with a much nicer environment.

I found a highly rated café nearby. When I first went in early May, the flame trees outside were in full bloom. After that, I almost always chose a window seat. Even after the flowering season, the trees remained lush and green. Whenever I looked up, there was a beautiful view.

I had thought about my career direction on and off before but never really worked it out. This time, I looked back at my career experience and the preferences behind several previous job choices.

Gradually, I realized that my career path over the years had already formed a clear direction, whether I had consciously planned it or not:

**Industrial B2B sales, focused on European and U.S. markets.**

Only then did I understand how to define and name the career direction I had already been following.

My resignation and job search followed naturally.

The idea of a digital garden also gradually emerged there. I began writing the earliest articles for my website. They look rough to me now, but for the first time, my digital garden began to take shape.

## **Slowing Down in Huizhou**

After leaving my job, I moved to Huizhou and temporarily stayed at a friend's place.

It took a little over ten minutes by bus to reach a nearby town.

It was not prosperous, but it had a strong sense of everyday life. The pace was slow, and I always enjoyed going there.

There was a spacious Starbucks there. The surrounding buildings were low, giving it an open, bright view, and outside was a wide street with little traffic. Sitting by the window, I felt deeply relaxed.

By then, most of my job-search preparation was done, so I spent more time on my digital garden.

Before finding my next job, I completed the first batch of articles across all the sections of my site.

During this period in Huizhou, I also decided on my next job. Once that was settled, this short transition period ended.

## **Revisiting the Past**

In mid-August, I joined a new company and moved to Dongguan.

After years of working, this was the first time I truly had weekends off, and I was incredibly excited.

I spent some weekends adjusting to my new city and life, and the rest on my digital garden.

I found a nearby Starbucks Reserve on the outer edge of a large shopping mall. The surroundings were not as open or quiet as at some other cafés, but the interior was comfortable, the music was good, and I still felt relaxed there.

It was there that I reopened the articles I had written in July.

I realized that because I had little experience writing this kind of content at the time, and because I had been too worried about revealing information about my former company, many of the articles were too shallow.

So I began revising those articles and going back through my past customer cases and career experiences. I spent many weekends there doing this.

Later, when I traveled back to Huizhou and passed through Bao'an District in Shenzhen, I would sometimes get off the metro halfway and go to another Starbucks nearby.

The mall was not crowded, and the Starbucks had so much indoor and outdoor space that it almost felt luxurious. People were scattered around, and almost every time, I had an entire row of long tables to myself.

I would often sit there and continue revising my articles and working through my customer cases.

Reconstructing the timeline.

Recovering the facts.

Identifying the judgments I had actually made at the time.

Then drawing out my own understanding from those facts.

Eventually, an article that I felt was truly worthy of carrying the name **Yolanda Jiang** slowly took shape.

That process gave me a strong sense of accomplishment.

## **Recording This Year**

Now, when I occasionally go back to Huizhou, I still make a point of going to the Starbucks in that little town to experience its slower pace again.

Next May, I would like to return to the café near the community where I once lived and see the flame trees outside the window in full bloom again.

This has been a very quiet year for me.

I did not have much of a social life. On many weekends, it was simply me, my laptop, and a café.

But during this same year, I reorganized the way I approached my work and began noticing some of my long-standing personality patterns and habits.

I reconsidered my career direction, went through a resignation and a job search, and built my digital garden from scratch.

Looking back, it feels almost like a year in which I rebuilt my life.

I cleared away many small things that had occupied my attention for a long time, while also building new systems that were truly worth keeping for the long term.

At the same time, I have started reminding myself:

I have spent enough time sorting things out.

Compared with a year ago, I feel much lighter and have a clearer sense of direction.

What I need next is to go out into the world more.

To work. To do more. To meet real people. And to build more connections in real life.

Maybe I no longer need to go to cafés so often to sort out my life.

**But I want to record this year.**
