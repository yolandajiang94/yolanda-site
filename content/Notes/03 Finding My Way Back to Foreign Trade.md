After leaving my first company in 2020, I moved into a domestic sales role in the health management industry, mainly because I was interested in the industry. I had not thought carefully about whether the role itself suited me.

### **A Different Kind of Sales**

Compared with foreign trade, this role required a very different way of developing customers. It relied heavily on personal networking, social relationships, and offline customer development.

I struggled with this way of working. Instead of developing customers independently, I often helped with internal tasks or accompanied more experienced colleagues when they visited customers.

### **Staying for Ten Months**

I probably realized within the first few months that the role was not a good fit, but I was reluctant to admit that I might have made the wrong choice.

I had just changed industries, and leaving too soon felt like giving up. So I stayed for around ten months.

During that period, an experienced colleague noticed that I was struggling. She said:

_“Maybe you should go back to foreign trade. I think it suits you better.”_

Her words were important to me at the time. I had started to wonder whether I was simply not good at sales, but her suggestion helped me see another possibility: perhaps I was just not suited to a sales model built heavily around personal networking and social relationships.

### **Returning to Foreign Trade**

Eventually, I decided to leave the health management industry and return to foreign trade.

By then, I had started to understand that foreign trade itself might not have been the problem. During the last two years of my first job, I had worked mainly as an assistant to a key account manager. I could handle the work, but constantly working around major customers and large projects was not a working style I wanted in the long term.

When I returned to foreign trade, I chose to work as a salesperson again rather than in a supporting role. I wanted to develop customers independently and have more ownership over my work. 

That decision became the beginning of the next stage of my career.
