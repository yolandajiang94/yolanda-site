# **Learning What Truly Deserves My Attention**

For many years, I knew that I paid a lot of attention to my belongings.

I noticed that I spent a significant amount of time organizing, maintaining, and taking care of small things in my daily life.

But I never truly understood the cost behind this habit.

In my mind, there was always a reason to defend it.

I told myself:

“This is just who I am.”

“I am someone who finds happiness and comfort in small things.”

“Even if an object is not expensive, it belongs to me. Why shouldn’t I care about it?”

“I am not hurting anyone. This way of living feels right to me.”

And I firmly followed this instinct.

At that time, I believed I was simply being true to myself.

---

## **When A Strength Becomes A Burden**

Looking back now, I realize that the problem was not caring about things.

There is nothing wrong with appreciating small moments, taking care of belongings, or finding comfort in everyday life.

The problem was that I gave almost everything the same level of attention.

A small kitchen tool, a cheap accessory, an ordinary household item — if it belonged to me, it could occupy my thoughts just like something truly important.

I treated every small uncertainty as something worth solving.

But attention is not unlimited.

Time is not unlimited.

Energy is not unlimited.

At 32, when I looked back at my life, career, and savings, I had to ask myself a difficult question:

Why have I worked so hard for so many years, yet accumulated so little?

If I continued living in the same way, would the next ten years look any different?

That was when I started admitting something I had avoided:

I had been living in a way that was much more exhausting than I realized.

---

## **Moving Helped Me See Things Differently**

For eight years after graduating, from 2017 to early 2026, I only moved three times.

And those moves were not even major relocations.

I either moved to another room in the same building or across the street.

Even then, I always felt moving was a huge project.

Because for someone like me, moving was never just moving.

It meant sorting through every item I owned.

Every object carried a history.

Every decision required attention.

But from March to July 2026, I moved three times.

Later, after finding a new job, I will move again.

Surprisingly, I no longer see moving as such a difficult thing.

Because through repeated experience, I finally understood what truly matters.

The most important things are actually very limited.

First, there is my document box.

It contains important certificates and personal documents. This is something I always carry myself during a move.

Second, there are my electronic devices — my laptop and phone.

These are my tools for work and learning, so I keep them close in my backpack.

Then there are my clothes, skincare products, and cosmetics.

These are items I have carefully selected over time. They have practical value and are worth keeping.

Everything else?

The boxes filled with kitchenware, household supplies, and miscellaneous items?

They are replaceable.

If something is lost, I can simply buy it again.

---

## **Letting Go Does Not Mean I Care Less**

The biggest change is that I no longer need to protect every single thing with the same level of attention.

I do not have to spend days searching for a missing pair of scissors.

I do not have to treat every misplaced object as evidence that something is wrong.

When I think this way, I feel much lighter.

For a long time, I thought letting go meant becoming careless.

Now I understand that letting go is actually a way of taking better care of myself.

---

## **Understanding Value Beyond Consumption**

This change also made me rethink my relationship with consumption.

In my twenties, like many people, I bought many things because I wanted to explore, try, and experience.

But looking back, many purchases did not create lasting value.

The most valuable purchase I made in recent years was actually quite simple.

In July 2024, I bought my current laptop.

After government subsidy, it cost a little over 5,000 RMB.

Before that, my old computer was extremely slow, and I often avoided using it after work because every task took too much effort.

This new laptop changed my life.

It became my tool for learning, working, and thinking.

Over the past year, I used it to learn with GPT, organize my thoughts, and build my personal website.

It was not just a device.

It became a productivity tool that supported my growth.

---

## **Choosing Where My Attention Goes**

I do not think my past self was wrong.

At that time, caring about small things gave me comfort.

It helped me create a sense of stability when many other parts of life felt uncertain.

But as life moves forward, the same habit can become a limitation.

Growth is not always about adding more.

Sometimes it is about removing what quietly consumes our energy.

I am learning that not everything deserves equal attention.

Some things deserve my care.

Some things deserve my investment.

Some things can simply be replaced.

My attention is one of my most valuable resources.

And learning where to place it may be one of the most important changes I have made.
