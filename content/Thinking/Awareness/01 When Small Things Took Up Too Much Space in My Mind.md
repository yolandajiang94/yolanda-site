# **When Small Things Took Up Too Much Space in My Mind**

For a long time, I did not realize that my attention to my belongings was not simply about being careful or organized.

It came from a deeper need for control.

For anything that belonged to me, I wanted to know where it was, how it was kept, and whether everything was still within my awareness.

A small object was never just a small object.

When something went missing, what bothered me was not its value. What troubled me was the uncertainty behind it — the feeling that something had slipped beyond my understanding and control.

I treated almost everything related to me with the same level of attention, regardless of its value.

A notebook, a kitchen tool, a small accessory — if it was mine, I felt responsible for keeping track of it.

At first, I thought this was simply part of my personality.

Later, I realized that this habit was quietly consuming my attention and energy.

---

## **The Missing Scissors**

In March 2025, I moved out of the urban village where I had lived for several years and moved into an apartment.

During the move, I received some help packing my belongings.

After moving, I spent more than a week organizing everything. Every box was unpacked, every bag was sorted, and my new home gradually became tidy.

But I could not find one small item:

A black pair of scissors from IKEA.

It cost only 2.99 RMB.

It was not valuable, but I used it almost every day — opening packages, cutting labels, and handling small things around the house.

I felt strangely upset.

It was not because I lost a cheap pair of scissors.

What bothered me was the feeling that something was out of my control.

I started questioning myself:

“Why don’t I remember where I put it?”

“Does this mean my memory is getting worse?”

“If I can lose track of this, are there other things in my life that are also beyond my control?”

A small missing object became connected with a much bigger concern.

I kept thinking about it for a long time.

More than two weeks later, I opened a storage box where I kept my skincare products.

There it was.

The scissors had been carefully placed there.

They were not lost.

They were simply placed somewhere I did not know.

At that moment, I finally relaxed.

The problem was never that I was careless.

The situation just had an explanation that I had not known.

Once the story became complete, my mind could finally let go.

---

## **The Missing Clips**

A year later, something similar happened again.

In April 2026, I moved again, this time into a community that I had been looking forward to for a long time.

During this move, I was mainly responsible for packing my own belongings.

After settling in, I realized that I could not find my food sealing clips.

I tried to recall where I had put them.

My usual assumption was:

“If something is still useful, I probably would not throw it away.”

But another possibility appeared:

“Maybe I really did throw them away when I was trying to simplify my belongings.”

The uncertainty appeared again.

But this time, something was different.

I noticed the anxiety.

I noticed the urge to keep searching and replaying the situation in my mind.

And I chose not to continue.

I told myself:

“They are just a few clips.”

“If I cannot find them, I can replace them.”

So I bought a new set of IKEA sealing clips online.

It cost less than 10 RMB.

And I moved on.

---

Three months later, when I was preparing to transfer my apartment lease, a new tenant came to view the place with her mother.

They paid close attention to details and checked almost every corner of the apartment.

While looking through the kitchen, they opened a drawer that I had never used.

Inside were my missing sealing clips.

The story finally had an ending.

The clips were never lost.

The cleaning lady we hired after moving in had placed them in that drawer while helping organize the apartment.

But what surprised me was not the discovery itself.

It was my reaction.

A year earlier, I had spent many days thinking about a missing object.

This time, even before finding the clips, I had already accepted the possibility that they might never appear again.

---

## **Learning Where to Place My Attention**

Looking back, I realize that my problem was never about caring for my belongings.

Being responsible and organized are valuable qualities.

The problem was that I treated every small uncertainty as something that required my attention.

A missing pair of scissors was not just a missing pair of scissors.

It became a question about my ability to manage my life.

A misplaced item became evidence that something might be wrong.

But life is full of uncertainty.

Things get misplaced.

Plans change.

People forget.

Not everything needs to be solved immediately.

Over the past year, one of the biggest changes I have noticed in myself is learning to distinguish between what deserves my attention and what does not.

My energy is limited.

My attention is limited.

And how I choose to use them shapes the life I build.

Sometimes growth is not about becoming more disciplined or more careful.

Sometimes growth is about learning what you can finally let go of.
