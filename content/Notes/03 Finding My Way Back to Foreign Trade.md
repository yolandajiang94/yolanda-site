# **Finding My Way Back to Foreign Trade Understanding What Fits Me**

After leaving my first company in 2020, I decided to try something completely different.

I moved into a domestic sales role in the health management industry.

At that time, the decision was mainly driven by personal interest.

I was curious about the industry, but I did not think deeply enough about whether the role itself matched my personality, strengths, and working style.

Soon, I realized that different sales roles require very different abilities.

---

## **A Career Choice That Did Not Fit**

Compared with foreign trade, domestic sales required a very different way of working.

The role depended heavily on:

· frequent social interaction;

· building personal relationships;

· proactively developing customers through offline communication.

I struggled in this environment.

It was not because I did not want to work hard.

The problem was that the way this role created business opportunities did not match my strengths.

I was not good at approaching unfamiliar people without a clear starting point, maintaining relationships mainly through social interaction, or constantly looking for new connections.

Instead of developing customers independently, I often helped with internal tasks or accompanied experienced colleagues when they visited customers.

As time passed, I became increasingly uncertain.

I did not know what I should focus on or whether I was moving in the right direction.

---

## **Why I Stayed Longer Than I Should Have**

Looking back, the biggest challenge at that time was not only choosing the wrong direction.

It was my unwillingness to admit that I might have made a mistake.

I had just changed industries.

If I left too soon, I felt it meant that I had failed or given up too easily.

So I kept telling myself:

"Maybe I just need more time."

In reality, I probably should have made the decision within two or three months.

But I stayed for around ten months because I did not want to accept that my choice was not suitable.

---

## **A Different Perspective From an Experienced Colleague**

During that period, an older colleague noticed my situation.

She saw that I often looked confused and did not know what I should do every day.

Instead of telling me to simply keep trying, she gave me another perspective.

She said:

_"Maybe you should go back to foreign trade. I think it suits you better."_

At that moment, her words were important.

Because I was starting to believe that the problem was me.

I thought:

"Maybe I am just not capable of doing sales."

But she helped me see another possibility.

Maybe I was not incapable.

Maybe I was simply in a role that did not match my strengths.

---

## **Understanding Myself Through Experience**

Looking back today, I understand her advice better.

My previous experience had already helped me build valuable abilities:

· following projects;

· communicating with customers;

· coordinating different teams;

· managing details;

· moving long-term tasks forward.

These abilities are especially important in B2B sales, where success often depends on patience, professional communication, and long-term trust.

The problem was not that I was unsuitable for sales.

I was simply not suitable for every type of sales.

---

Eventually, I decided to leave the health management industry and return to foreign trade.

This time, I was not returning because of the idealized image I had when I was a student.

I was returning with a better understanding of myself.

I learned that career choices are not only about choosing a promising industry.

They are also about understanding:

· what kind of work matches your personality;

· what abilities you naturally build;

· what environment allows you to perform at your best.

Sometimes, the right direction is not discovered through perfect planning.

It is discovered through experience, mistakes, and the courage to adjust.

My journey back to foreign trade was not a step backward.

It was a step toward a career path that better matched who I was.
