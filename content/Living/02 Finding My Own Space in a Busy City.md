# **Finding My Own Space in a Busy City**

For many years, I have had a simple weekend habit:

Finding a comfortable café and spending some quiet time there.

Interestingly, I am not particularly interested in coffee itself.

What attracts me is the space.

The light.

The atmosphere.

The feeling of being surrounded by people while still having my own quiet corner.

For someone who has lived alone for many years, this kind of space has become an important part of my life.

---

## **I Needed More Than a Quiet Place**

After graduating from university, I moved to Shenzhen and have spent most of my adult life living alone.

I have had relationships, but most of the time, I have been on my own.

For many years, I never saw this as a problem.

Actually, I often felt that I had too little time.

Work.

Daily responsibilities.

Household tasks.

There was always something waiting to be done.

Especially in my earlier years, I lived in relatively simple conditions.

Although I always tried to keep my life organized:

working out regularly,

cooking for myself,

and maintaining a comfortable living environment,

staying at home all weekend could still make me feel trapped.

Later, I gradually realized something:

People do not only need a private place to live.

They also need a public space where they can stay connected with the outside world.

This connection does not always require conversation.

I do not need to know everyone around me.

But sitting in a comfortable café, seeing people studying, working, or simply enjoying their own time, gives me a sense of connection.

I am still alone.

But I am not completely isolated.

---

## **A Year of Thinking in Cafés**

Over the past year, I went to cafés more often than ever before.

One important reason was my frequent conversations with GPT.

During this period, cafés became a place where I organized my thoughts and reflected on my life.

I reviewed my work in foreign trade.

I reconsidered my career direction.

I explored my relationship patterns.

I reflected on my personality and the experiences that shaped me.

For a long time, I rarely had the space to think deeply about these questions.

Not because I did not want to.

But because my energy was mostly spent dealing with daily life.

When a person is constantly solving immediate problems, it is difficult to step back and observe their own life.

Over the past year, as my living environment improved and many daily burdens became lighter, I suddenly had more space for reflection.

And for the first time, I started to understand another side of living alone:

Sometimes, it can feel lonely.

---

## **Understanding What I Could Not Understand Before**

A few years ago, I had a friend who decided to leave a big city and return to her hometown because she could not adapt to the loneliness of urban life.

At that time, I could not fully understand her decision.

For me, the city still represented opportunities.

I had many things I wanted to achieve.

I always felt busy.

Loneliness was never my biggest concern.

But this year, I started to understand that feeling.

Living alone gives me freedom.

I can arrange my time in my own way.

I can have my own space and follow my own rhythm.

But at the same time, a life built entirely around oneself requires more active effort to stay connected with the world.

Life cannot only be about thinking, analyzing, and improving.

People also need new environments, new experiences, and new connections.

---

## **Still Exploring the Life I Want**

Recently, I have been going through a transition period.

I left my previous job and am exploring the next stage of my career.

Many things are still uncertain.

I do not know exactly where I will live in the future or what kind of routine I will build.

But I have become increasingly aware of one thing:

The environment around us shapes the way we live.

A beautiful café.

A comfortable neighborhood.

A workplace where we can meet new people.

These ordinary things quietly influence the quality of our daily lives.

For many years, I focused mainly on solving problems.

Now, I am learning how to create a life that I genuinely enjoy.

Maybe this is also a part of growing up.

Finding not only a place to stay,

but a place where I feel connected to the world.
