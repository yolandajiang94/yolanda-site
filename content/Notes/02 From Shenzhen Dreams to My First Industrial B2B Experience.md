# **From Shenzhen Dreams to My First Industrial B2B Experience**

In 2017, after graduating from university, I moved from Wuhan to Shenzhen.

At that time, Shenzhen represented opportunity and possibility to me.

I came from a rural family, and like many young people from smaller cities, I hoped that through education and work, I could build a different future.

During university, many students from my school chose the Pearl River Delta region as their first career destination. As an economics student, I often heard that Shenzhen was a city full of opportunities, especially for people working in foreign trade.

There was also another simple reason that attracted me.

I grew up in Hubei, where winters are cold and damp. Every winter morning, getting out of bed required a lot of courage. When I heard that Shenzhen had warm winters, sunshine, and blue skies, the city naturally became part of my imagination of a better life.

So after graduation, I came to Shenzhen with two close friends by train.

At that time, I did not know exactly what my future would look like.

But I believed that Shenzhen was a place where I could start.

---

## **My First Step Into Industrial B2B Sales**

Before graduation, I found my first job through campus recruitment.

It was an LED lighting company serving industrial lighting markets, and I joined as a foreign trade salesperson after receiving my graduation certificate in June 2017.

When I was a university student majoring in International Economics and Trade, I had imagined foreign trade as an international and professional career.

I imagined communicating with overseas customers, understanding different cultures, traveling abroad for business, and becoming someone who worked in a global environment.

However, the reality of foreign trade was much more complicated than I had imagined.

I quickly realized that being a foreign trade salesperson was not simply about English skills or knowledge of international trade.

For a fresh graduate, developing customers independently was extremely challenging.

It required:

· understanding markets;

· finding potential business opportunities;

· communicating effectively with customers;

· building trust over time;

· handling uncertainty and rejection.

At that stage, I had enthusiasm, but I lacked experience and practical skills.

What I did not fully understand at that time was that I had already entered the world of industrial B2B sales.

The products I worked with were not consumer goods.

They involved business customers, technical requirements, project discussions, and long-term cooperation.

Although I did not have a clear understanding of B2B sales at that stage, this experience became the foundation of my later career.

---

## **My First Encounter With Real Business**

The company placed strong expectations on new graduates.

More than ten fresh employees joined at the same time, and we went through intensive training. We practiced presentations almost every day and were constantly pushed to improve our communication skills.

At that stage, I was full of energy.

I wanted to prove myself.

A few months after joining the company, I received an opportunity that seemed extremely exciting at the time.

In September 2017, the company arranged for another colleague and me to spend six months in the United States to explore potential business opportunities.

An experienced colleague from the company went with us, mainly to provide support during our time abroad and help us with practical issues.

However, business development still depended largely on ourselves.

At that time, we were fresh graduates with limited sales experience. We had the courage to try, but we did not yet understand how to independently develop overseas markets, communicate with potential customers, and turn conversations into real business opportunities.

The six months in the United States did not bring the business progress we had hoped for.

For a long time, I blamed myself.

I felt that I had failed to make good use of the opportunity and disappointed the trust the company had given me.

But looking back today, I see that experience differently.

It was not simply a failure.

It was the first time I clearly understood the gap between enthusiasm and capability.

Having an opportunity does not mean that you are already prepared for it.

Sometimes, real growth begins when we recognize the distance between where we are and where we want to be.

---

## **Discovering My Strengths Through Another Role**

After returning to China, I gradually realized that independent customer development was not something I could master immediately.

Fortunately, the company was expanding its business with brand customers, and a newly promoted key account manager needed an assistant to support customer projects and daily coordination.

I accepted the role.

This change became an important stage in my early career.

Instead of constantly struggling with how to find new customers, I had clearer responsibilities:

· following up on customer projects;

· communicating with clients;

· coordinating internal teams;

· tracking details and progress;

· supporting longer sales cycles.

During those two years, I did not become an expert at customer acquisition.

However, I developed another set of abilities that later became valuable throughout my career:

· project follow-up;

· customer communication;

· cross-functional coordination;

· keeping complex tasks moving forward.

These experiences gradually shaped the way I understood sales.

Sales is not only about finding customers.

It is also about understanding needs, solving problems, coordinating resources, and building trust through continuous communication.

---

## **Why I Left My First Job**

However, the working environment during that period was extremely demanding.

The pace was fast, and overtime was common.

I looked at my manager, who was also working late every day, and started questioning whether this was the working style I wanted in the long term.

At that time, I did not dislike foreign trade itself.

What I questioned was whether this specific working rhythm was sustainable for me.

In April 2020, after nearly three years in my first company, I decided to leave and explore another direction.

At that time, I thought I was moving away from international B2B sales.

But years later, I realized that I was not leaving this field.

I was still searching for a way of working that better matched my strengths.

---

Looking back, my first job in Shenzhen was much more than the beginning of my foreign trade career.

It was my first experience in industrial B2B sales.

It taught me how businesses communicate, how projects move forward, and how trust is gradually built between companies.

More importantly, it helped me discover the parts of sales that I truly enjoy.

This experience became the foundation for the next stage of my career.
