After graduating in June 2017, I moved to Shenzhen.

During my first year of work, I lived in a company dormitory near an industrial park. At the time, I paid little attention to my living environment, as work and gaining experience felt much more important.

In August 2018, my department moved to Futian, and I started renting a place on my own.

I rented a typical single-room apartment in an urban village in Baishilong, Longhua. There was no real window in the main room, so I often had to turn on the lights even during the day.

The so-called kitchen was more like a narrow passageway with a small area set aside for cooking. The room was completely empty when I first rented it. I bought a bed online and assembled it myself.

Looking back now, it was certainly not a comfortable living environment.

But at the time, I did not feel disappointed.

## **Making the Best of What I Had**

For years after that, I never seriously thought about improving my living environment.

I had always enjoyed living alone and never seriously considered sharing an apartment. Although the urban-village apartment was simple, it gave me a private space of my own and suited my financial situation at the time.

Back then, I always believed one thing:

**As long as I kept my room clean and organized, I could still live a good life.**

On weekends, I would clean the room, do my laundry, organize my things, and try to make myself as comfortable as possible within the conditions I had.

I would also go to the nearby market, buy fresh vegetables and meat, and come home to cook a proper meal for myself.

At the time, I did not feel that anything was missing from my life.

In March 2021, I moved to another apartment in the same urban village. It was still a fairly basic single room, but it had slightly better natural light.

In March 2023, I moved again, this time into a one-bedroom apartment in the same building. I had more space, but the overall living environment did not fundamentally change.

I had simply become used to the idea that “good enough was enough.” A smaller room or less natural light was something I could accept, as long as I had a clean and comfortable space of my own.

Only years later did I realize how used I had become to adapting to what I had. I rarely asked myself:

**If I had more choices, what kind of place would I actually want to live in every day?**

## **When the Space Started Supporting My Life**

In March 2025, I finally moved out of the urban village where I had lived for many years.

I did not leave Baishilong. I simply increased my rental budget and moved into an apartment building.

The apartment itself was brighter and cleaner. I no longer needed to spend so much time on weekends cleaning and organizing just to make up for the limitations of the space itself.

For the first time, I clearly felt that:

**Instead of constantly trying to make the space work for my life, the space itself was starting to support my life.**

Around the same time, my work became less intense than in earlier years. I gradually had more time, energy, and mental space to pay attention to the place where I lived every day.

I had already lived in Baishilong for many years, yet I rarely ate there and hardly knew the small restaurants and shops around me. I passed many of those places every day without really noticing them.

In 2025, I began exploring the small restaurants nearby, noticing the shops and fruit stalls along the street, and finding places I genuinely liked and returned to regularly.

Those streets and shops had always been there.

I simply had not had much time or mental space to notice them before.

## **Finding What I Wanted in a Home**


<img src="/images/balcony-view.jpg" alt="View from my balcony" width="800">



In April 2026, I left Baishilong, where I had lived for many years, and moved from Longhua to Bantian in Longgang.

This time, I moved into a residential community I had been looking forward to living in for a long time.

The entire community consists of low-rise buildings, with no high-rise residential towers, and the overall density is relatively low. Mature trees, flowers, and green spaces are everywhere.

I live on the fourth floor.

The apartment is relatively spacious, with a square and open layout and plenty of natural light. The living room connects to two big open balconies facing different directions, and even the windows in the kitchen and bathroom look out onto trees and greenery. From almost anywhere in the apartment, I can see greenery outside. Sometimes it feels as if the entire home is surrounded by green.

When friends saw the photos I took, some of them even thought I was living in a villa.

By then, I had become much clearer about the kind of living environment I wanted:

**A spacious home with plenty of natural light, a square and open layout, a low-rise residential community, and a good natural environment.**

I am not particularly drawn to living in the city center, nor do I care much about luxurious interiors or expensive furniture.

What matters more to me is sunlight, open space, trees outside my windows, and the feeling that I can truly relax when I come home.

In the morning, sunlight comes into the apartment. After work, I return to a community filled with trees, flowers, and open space. At the entrance gate, an automated voice says, “Welcome home,” and the security guards often greet residents as we come and go.

After dinner, I often walk around the community, pass through the small commercial street, and stop by the supermarket to look at the vegetables and fruit. Sometimes the vegetables marked down in the evening cost only a few yuan.

These are all very ordinary things. But I notice them now, and I genuinely feel that they matter to me.

## **What Home Means to Me Now**

As my life changed and I had more choices, my understanding of home gradually expanded as well.

In the past, home was mainly a place to sleep and rest, as well as a private space that belonged entirely to me. That part has not really changed. But now I also care about how the space itself makes me feel.

The environment itself can also be a form of support.

**And now, “home” no longer ends at my front door.**

It also includes the trees outside my windows, the community I walk through every day, the familiar shops nearby, and the ordinary little things that have become part of my daily life.
