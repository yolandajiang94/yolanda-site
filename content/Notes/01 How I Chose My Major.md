In the summer of 2013, I had to make one of the first important decisions about my future: choosing a university and a major.

I grew up in a rural area and had limited access to information or guidance about universities and careers at the time. I did not own a computer and rarely had access to the internet. Most of the information I could find came from occasional visits to internet cafés or the computer rooms at school.

My college entrance exam score was only two points below the cutoff for the first undergraduate admission category. When choosing a university, I wanted to make the best use of my score while finding a direction that interested me.

At first, I considered studying outside Hubei Province because I wanted to experience a different environment, but eventually decided to stay in Wuhan. One option I looked at was fashion design at Wuhan Textile University. It was a well-known program, but I gradually realized that I was more attracted to the reputation of the major than to the work itself.

Then I came across International Economics and Trade at Hubei University of Economics.

I did not really understand international trade at the time. What attracted me was the professional life I imagined behind it.

I pictured myself communicating with overseas clients, traveling for business, working in an international environment, and becoming a professional business person connected with the wider world. I had also heard that foreign trade salespeople could earn commissions, which made the career seem full of possibilities.

So I chose International Economics and Trade.

Looking back, I knew very little about what foreign trade work actually involved when I made that decision. I was mostly attracted by an imagined professional identity.

But that choice became the starting point of my career. After graduating in 2017, I entered foreign trade and eventually built my career in industrial B2B sales.
