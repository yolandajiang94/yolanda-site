# **Building Trust Before the Opportunity Appears**

## **How Consistent Customer Development Creates Long-Term Business Opportunities**

In industrial B2B sales, opportunities do not always appear immediately after the first contact.

Many potential customers may not have an active project when you first reach out.

They may not reply.

They may not have a budget.

They may simply not need the product at that moment.

However, this does not mean the relationship has no value.

For long-cycle industrial products, consistent and meaningful communication can create opportunities in the future.

---

## **Entering an Unfamiliar Market**

When I started exploring a new overseas academic market, I did not have an existing customer base or local network.

Instead of waiting for incoming inquiries, I built a targeted customer development process.

I focused on research groups related to:

· battery technology;

· electrochemistry;

· energy materials;

· energy storage.

Rather than contacting as many people as possible, I created a focused customer list based on application relevance.

The goal was not simply to send more emails.

The goal was to identify potential customers who could genuinely benefit from our products.

---

## **Consistency Creates Familiarity**

After building the target customer list, I maintained regular follow-up communication.

Many customers did not respond immediately.

This is normal in industrial B2B sales.

A researcher may receive your information today but only start considering new equipment months later.

During this period, my approach was not aggressive sales follow-up.

Instead, I focused on:

· maintaining visibility;

· sharing relevant information;

· understanding customer research direction;

· keeping communication professional and consistent.

Over time, these repeated interactions helped establish familiarity and trust.

---

## **When the Opportunity Appeared**

Several months after the initial contact, one research group that had not actively responded before reached out when they had a specific testing requirement.

Because we had already established communication history, the discussion started from a position of trust.

Instead of introducing our company from the beginning, we could directly focus on understanding:

· their research needs;

· testing requirements;

· suitable system configuration.

From there, I worked with the customer to define the right solution and coordinate the internal process toward the final order.

---

## **What I Learned About Customer Development**

This experience changed my understanding of business development.

Earlier in my career, I sometimes measured development effectiveness by immediate responses:

Did the customer reply?

Did the customer ask for a quotation?

Did the customer show purchasing intent?

However, industrial B2B sales often works differently.

A customer who does not respond today may still become an opportunity in the future.

The key is whether you have built enough trust and relevance before that moment arrives.

---

## **A Sustainable Development Approach**

For me, effective customer development is not simply:

Send more emails → Get more replies.

It is:

**Identify the right customers**

↓

**Understand their potential needs**

↓

**Maintain meaningful communication**

↓

**Build trust over time**

↓

**Be present when the opportunity appears**

---

## **What I Learned**

This experience strengthened my belief that industrial B2B sales is a long-term relationship-building process.

Customers do not only choose products.

They choose suppliers they already know and trust.

The ability to patiently develop relationships, even before a clear opportunity exists, is one of the most valuable skills I have developed in overseas B2B sales.
