For many years, I lived in the same area of Longhua, Shenzhen.

For about eight years, this area remained the center of my life. Work, home, and everything around me gradually settled into a stable routine.

That stability gave me a sense of security. Life could continue in familiar ways, and there often seemed to be no particular reason to reconsider it.

When everything becomes familiar, it is easy to simply keep living the same way.

Sometimes, we get so busy living our lives that we forget to ask ourselves: **Is the life we have built still the life we truly want?**

## **A Year When Everything Started to Change**

In 2026, several important parts of my life changed within just a few months.

Between March and July, I moved three times.

I ended a relationship.

And I left the company where I had worked for five years.

None of these changes happened according to some carefully designed plan. They simply happened one after another, gradually changing the way I thought about stability.

In the past, moving away from a place where I had lived for a long time felt like a major decision. But after moving several times within a few months, I realized that leaving a familiar home and adjusting to a new place was not as difficult as I had imagined.

A relationship that I had taken seriously and expected to continue also came to an end. It had a profound impact on me. It made me realize more clearly that even the things in life that once felt important and stable were not necessarily unchangeable.

For a long time, many parts of my life had remained unchanged because changing them required energy and courage, and meant facing uncertainty.

But when my home, relationship, and work all changed within a matter of months, I began to realize:

**Maybe my life was not as fixed as I had imagined.**

## **Looking Beyond Shenzhen**

This year, I also began thinking more seriously about my long-term career.

For years, I had naturally connected my career with Shenzhen. I had lived and worked here for a long time. It was familiar, and there were always plenty of job opportunities.

But this year, as I began considering a job change, I took a closer look at the industrial B2B sales opportunities available in the market. I found that there were fewer opportunities in Shenzhen that genuinely matched the direction I wanted to pursue than I had expected.

That led me to a question I had rarely considered seriously before:

**If the right opportunity is not in Shenzhen, do I really need to stay in Shenzhen?**

After leaving my previous company, I began looking at the next stage of my career across a wider area. Dongguan came into view, and I found opportunities there that were also aligned with the direction I wanted to take.

At the same time, the way I defined myself professionally began to change.

Instead of thinking of myself simply as someone working in overseas sales in Shenzhen, I began to see myself as an industrial B2B sales professional building a long-term career in the Pearl River Delta.

That perspective was larger than Shenzhen itself.

The cities across the Pearl River Delta are not completely separate worlds. Manufacturing, business opportunities, transportation, and everyday life already move across city boundaries.

And once I started thinking about my career in terms of the region rather than a single city, my idea of where I could build a life began to expand as well.

## **Experiencing a Sense of Movement**

After leaving my job in July, I temporarily left Shenzhen and stayed in Huizhou.

In early August, I spent a weekend with a friend in Huicheng, the central urban area of Huizhou. My friend left on Sunday, but I decided to stay for another day and explore the city on my own.

I walked around different neighborhoods, looked for local restaurants, took buses and taxis, and went to places I had wanted to visit but never quite made time for before.

What stayed with me was not any particular attraction.

It was simply the feeling of finally having the time and space to experience a place without needing to rush anywhere.

I saw rivers running through the city and walked along streets lined with local restaurants that had been there for years.

On the third evening, I took a rideshare from Huicheng back to Daya Bay, where I was temporarily staying. The two places were about 50 kilometers apart.

As I sat in the car and watched the streets and urban landscapes change outside the window, I felt a very particular sense of movement.

I had just left a job I had been in for five years, and it had often been stressful. For the moment, I felt much lighter, with more space to think about where I wanted to go next.

I did not yet know where my next job would be, and for the moment, there was no workplace I needed to return to every day.

Strangely, that uncertainty did not feel as unsettling as I once thought it would.

**Sitting in that car, I felt as if I could go anywhere.**

It made the Pearl River Delta feel different to me. These cities did not have to be separate choices, with one becoming permanent and all the others left outside my life.

Life could unfold across them.

## **Looking Forward**

For a long time, I thought stability meant staying in one place. Now I am beginning to understand that stability can also come from having the ability to adapt and build a new routine when things change.

The past several months have been a period of saying goodbye:

to an old home,  
to an old relationship,  
to an old career stage.

But they have also been a period of discovering new possibilities.

It is now early August 2026, and I do not know exactly where I will work or live next. Maybe it will be Shenzhen, another city nearby, or somewhere I have not considered yet.

But I no longer feel the same need to find one permanent answer immediately. What matters more is knowing that I have the ability to choose, adjust, and explore.

After years of building a life around familiarity, I am learning that a meaningful life is not only about finding a place and staying there.

**It is also about keeping the freedom to move forward.**
