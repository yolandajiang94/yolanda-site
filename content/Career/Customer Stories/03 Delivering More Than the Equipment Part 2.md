## **Beyond Delivery**

### **Two More Years Supporting the Same Research Laboratory**

## **Delivery Was Not the End**

In 2024, when the equipment was installed and the laboratory began using the systems in real research, this collaboration entered another stage.

From a sales process perspective, the equipment had already been delivered.

But for the customer, delivery was not the end.

The real research had just started.

Once equipment moves from a specification on a quotation sheet to an actual research tool used in the laboratory, new questions and new application needs naturally appear over time.

After installation, the laboratory entered the daily operation stage.

During the early period of equipment usage, I continued helping the customer address software and hardware-related questions, working with our service engineers when technical support was needed.

Because the main contact was a Chinese researcher at the laboratory, I created a WeChat communication group to make daily communication more efficient.

When the customer encountered an issue, I would first understand the actual situation, then coordinate with the appropriate technical team and arrange remote support when necessary.

The focus was no longer only about completing a delivery.

It was about helping the customer make the equipment work effectively in their actual research environment.

## **Understanding the Application Behind a New Testing Requirement**

In October 2024, after the equipment had been running in the laboratory for some time, the research team developed a new testing requirement.

They needed to test a 161Ah pouch cell with 2C charge and discharge, requiring approximately 322A current.

This was not a new equipment purchase.

Instead, the customer wanted to use the existing 5V200A system they had already purchased and asked:

Was there a fixture suitable for approximately 300A testing?

The customer had already found a fixture on our website but was unsure whether it would fit their actual application.

At first glance, this looked like a simple accessory request:

Finding a 300A fixture.

However, after understanding the testing condition in more detail, I realized that the question was not only about finding a fixture.

The customer had already considered connecting two 200A channels in parallel to achieve the required current.

The remaining challenge was how to make the high-current connection reliable and practical.

Because the original high-current cables were thick, directly connecting multiple cables together was not an ideal solution. The physical connection space and actual operation needed further consideration.

The solution gradually changed from:

“Find a 300A fixture”

into:

Using the existing 200A channels in parallel, together with suitable fixtures and additional connection components, to create a practical testing setup.

## **Turning an Accessory Request into a Practical Solution**

After further reviewing the connection method, we found that a more suitable connection design was needed.

When two high-current channels are connected in parallel, multiple large cables need to be connected to the fixture at the same time. A direct connection would leave limited space and make operation less convenient.

Therefore, our engineering team designed a customized copper busbar solution.

The purpose of the busbar was to provide a more practical connection structure for the parallel high-current setup, making the overall solution better suited for actual use.

This was not simply a matter of selecting a standard accessory from inventory.

The copper busbar needed to be designed according to the actual connection method.

I submitted the requirements to engineering.

The engineering team prepared the drawing.

We confirmed the solution with the customer.

Then the customized parts entered production.

There were also some minor delays related to materials and production.

Eventually, the customized components were completed and delivered to the customer.

At the end of 2023, what arrived in Boston was:

8 crates of battery cyclers and chambers.

One year later, we shipped something else to the same laboratory.

This time, there was no large equipment shipment.

Only:

· specialized fixtures;

· customized copper busbars;

· related connection components.

The size was completely different.

The nature of the work was also different.

The first time, we helped a newly established laboratory build its testing capability.

This time, we helped an existing customer continue using their current system for new research needs.

## **When Equipment Becomes Part of the Customer’s Work**

After the accessories arrived in late November 2024, I followed up with the customer:

Had the fixtures arrived?

Did the copper busbar fit properly?

The customer replied that everything had been received and tested, and no issue was found at that moment.

However, later that same day, they sent another testing curve and reported a new phenomenon during actual operation.

So the communication continued.

This is actually a very common situation with industrial equipment.

Once equipment enters real application, one issue may be solved and experiments continue; as experiments continue, new questions may appear.

The equipment is no longer just a product delivered to the customer.

It becomes part of their ongoing work.

## **A Relationship That Continued Through Change**

From 2024 onward, my communication with this laboratory was no longer only about one purchase order.

It gradually became a long-term customer relationship.

The first researcher who was involved in the original equipment discussion continued working in the laboratory until 2026.

Starting from February 2024, another researcher became my main contact for daily communication regarding equipment usage.

Over more than one year, we continued discussing software operation, hardware situations, and issues encountered during actual research.

By March 2025, our communication was still ongoing. Later that year, he left the laboratory and moved to an industry position.

In March 2025, another researcher became the new contact person and continued communicating with me about the laboratory’s equipment.

From March 2025 until I left my previous company in 2026, we continued maintaining communication regarding the system.

Before my departure, I introduced the colleague who would take over my work, ensuring the laboratory could continue receiving support.

Over these years, the laboratory had gradually moved from a newly established research group planning its testing capability into a more stable research environment.

It no longer required the same level of frequent support as during the early operation stage.

But the original equipment continued serving their research.

New researchers joined.

New testing requirements appeared.

And the relationship evolved from an initial equipment purchase into ongoing support throughout the customer’s usage journey.

## **Looking Back: A Journey That Continued**

Looking back, more than three years had passed since I first contacted this newly established laboratory in 2022.

The professor had moved from building a new research group to operating a functioning laboratory.

The equipment had moved from models listed on a quotation sheet to research tools used over multiple years.

The people involved changed.

The research needs changed.

But the connection continued.

What started as:

Requirement → Procurement → Production → Delivery

gradually became:

Usage → Support → New Applications → Continued Support

The most valuable part of this experience was not solving one extraordinary problem.

Most individual tasks were ordinary.

But when looking back across the years:

2022 → 2023 → 2024 → 2025 → 2026

the laboratory changed.

The people changed.

The applications changed.

But the relationship never truly ended.
