I have always been someone who takes good care of the things I own.

To me, the value of an object is not entirely determined by how much it costs. Even if it is something small and inexpensive, once it becomes part of my life, I tend to give it some meaning. I want to take care of it, use it well, and make the most of it.

It was only over the past year or two that I gradually realized something: the problem was not that I cared too much about these things. It was that once something became meaningful to me, I tended to keep giving it my attention. As a result, things of very different value often received a similar amount of attention.

## **The Missing Scissors**

After moving in March 2025, I realized that I could not find a pair of black IKEA scissors.

They had cost only RMB 2.99. Of course I knew it would be easy to buy another pair, but I still couldn't just think, "Never mind."

Someone had helped me pack during the move. Afterward, I spent more than a week unpacking and organizing everything. The boxes were emptied, the bags were cleared out, and the new place gradually came together, but the scissors were still nowhere to be found.

I kept trying to remember where they could have gone. I even started questioning my memory. Why couldn't I remember at all? If something I used so often could simply disappear, were there other things that had also somehow slipped out of my control?

What bothered me was not losing something worth RMB 2.99. It was not knowing what had happened.

More than two weeks later, I opened a storage box for skincare products and finally found the scissors inside. Someone had simply put them there while helping me pack.

The moment I saw them, I felt relieved.

## **The Missing Clips**

After another move in April 2026, I realized that the food sealing clips I often used were missing.

Again, I started wondering where they could have gone. I normally would not throw away something that was still usable, but I had also been clearing things out around that time. Had I decided in the moment that I no longer needed them and thrown them away?

That familiar uncertainty came back. I could feel myself wanting to keep searching and going over everything again until I figured out exactly where the clips had gone.

But this time, after thinking about it for a day or two, I stopped.

I told myself they were just a few clips. If I really couldn't find them, I could buy new ones.

So I ordered another set of IKEA clips online for less than RMB 10, and then I moved on.

Three months later, I was preparing to sublet the apartment. A new tenant and her mother came to see the place. They looked around carefully and opened the kitchen drawers one by one. In a drawer I had never opened since moving in, I unexpectedly saw the missing clips.

It turned out that after I moved in, I had hired someone to clean the apartment, and she had put the clips into that drawer while tidying up.

They had never actually been lost.

## **The Soap Dish I Found Again**

I have always believed in a very simple idea: make full use of what you have. If something still works, I want to keep using it. There is something naturally satisfying to me about using an ordinary object well for many years.

This may also have something to do with how I grew up. I was a left-behind child and spent a lot of time alone when I was young. To me, familiar objects have never been just things I use.They also carry a sense of companionship and comfort.

When I left Shenzhen in July 2026, I moved everything I had accumulated over nine years to a friend's home in Huizhou. I started working in Dongguan in August, and at first I brought only the basic things I needed for daily life. Later, I would occasionally spend a weekend in Huizhou, sort through my belongings, and bring another batch of useful things back to Dongguan.

They were all very ordinary things: a bucket, a washbasin, a small trash bin I had used for many years, and a soap dish.

My friend said it would be easy to buy these things again in Dongguan.

My thinking was simple: **they are already here, and if I can keep using them, I might as well bring them with me.** 

While I was temporarily staying in Huizhou, I couldn't find that soap dish one day. I had wanted to use it, and when I realized it was missing, I did feel a little uncomfortable. But the feeling passed quickly. I told myself it was fine. It was just a small thing.

When I went back to Huizhou to sort through my belongings in September, I unexpectedly found it.

I was still pleasantly surprised and happy to see it. Now I could bring it to Dongguan and keep using it.

## **Keeping the Care Without Letting It Become a Burden**

In 2026, I started thinking more and more about one thing: how to make my life feel a little lighter.

I do not want to treat things as if they do not matter simply because they are inexpensive.

But I am beginning to understand that caring about something does not mean I have to keep giving it my attention. And something being meaningful does not mean it has to take up a lot of mental space.

Objects have their value, **and my attention has value too.**

So now I am more willing to remind myself:

Make full use of things, but don't take them too seriously.

Making full use of what I have can still be one of my values, but it does not have to remain a burden.
