# **When Explaining Became an Automatic Reaction**

For a long time, I believed that explanation was the best way to solve misunderstanding.

If someone did not understand my thoughts, my decisions, or my intentions, my first reaction was usually to explain more.

I would add more background.

More details.

More reasons.

I believed that if I could make my thoughts clear enough, the other person would eventually understand.

But over time, I started to notice something:

Sometimes I was not explaining because explanation was needed.

I was explaining because I felt uncomfortable with uncertainty.

---

## **The Habit of Making Things Clear**

I have always been someone who thinks deeply about things.

When something happens, I naturally try to understand the reasons behind it.

This ability has helped me in many areas of my life.

In work, it helps me analyze problems.

In relationships, it helps me consider different perspectives.

But this habit also has another side.

When I feel that something is unclear, my instinct is to solve it.

When I feel that someone might misunderstand me, my instinct is to correct it.

When I feel that someone might have concerns, my instinct is to remove those concerns.

Without realizing it, explanation became my automatic response.

---

## **When Communication Becomes Exhausting**

The problem was not that explanation was wrong.

Clear communication is important.

The problem was that I often did not know when to stop.

I treated every uncertainty as something that needed to be resolved.

I wanted to make sure:

· my intention was understood;

· my decision was reasonable;

· my words could not be misinterpreted;

· the other person would feel reassured.

So I kept adding more.

But sometimes, more explanation did not create more understanding.

Instead, it created a longer conversation, more emotions, and more things that needed to be explained.

Eventually, I found myself exhausted.

I was trying so hard to make things clear that I lost my own sense of calm.

---

## **The Hidden Reason Behind Over-Explaining**

Looking deeper, I realized that my need to explain came from two places.

The first was my desire to control uncertainty.

I wanted situations to have clear meanings.

I wanted problems to have clear solutions.

I wanted relationships to return to a stable state.

The second was my tendency to take responsibility for other people's feelings.

When I sensed that someone might feel uncertain or uncomfortable, I wanted to help them feel better.

I wanted to remove their worries.

I wanted them to feel completely reassured.

But I slowly realized:

Other people's emotions are not always problems that I need to solve.

---

## **Learning to Create a Pause**

Over the past year, I have started practicing something simple:

Pause before explaining.

Not every feeling of discomfort requires an immediate response.

Not every uncertainty needs to be removed.

Not every difference in understanding needs to be corrected.

I am learning to separate:

“I want to communicate clearly.”

from:

“I need the other person to fully understand me before I can feel okay.”

These two things may look similar, but they come from very different places.

---

## **Explanation as a Choice**

I do not want to become someone who never explains.

Explaining is still part of who I am.

I value clarity.

I value thoughtful communication.

I value understanding.

But I am learning to make explanation a choice, rather than an automatic reaction.

I can explain.

I can add context.

I can express myself.

But I can also stop.

I can allow some uncertainty to exist.

I can accept that sometimes people may only understand part of what I mean.

And that is okay.

The goal is not to say less.

The goal is to regain the ability to choose when to speak, how much to say, and when to let go.
