# **What I Am Looking For in My Next Career Stage**

After nearly ten years of working, I have started to think differently about career choices.

When I was younger, I often saw career decisions as a choice between different paths:

Which major should I choose?

Which industry should I enter?

Which company should I join?

I hoped that there was one perfect answer, and that finding it would allow me to move forward with confidence.

However, after experiencing different roles and working environments, I gradually realized that career development is rarely a straight line.

It is a process of making choices, gaining experience, adjusting direction, and understanding myself better.

As I enter the next stage of my career, I may not have every detail planned out, but I have a clearer understanding of what I want to continue building.

---

## **Continuing to Build in Industrial B2B Sales**

Over the past several years, through different roles in industrial B2B sales, I have gradually discovered where my strengths fit better.

From industrial lighting products to battery testing equipment, I have worked with different types of technical products and business customers.

Through these experiences, I have found that I enjoy a certain type of work:

· understanding customer needs;

· learning about technical products;

· communicating across different teams;

· following projects through different stages;

· building trust through long-term relationships.

Industrial B2B sales is rarely about quick transactions.

Many projects require time for customers to evaluate solutions, discuss technical details, coordinate internally, and gradually build confidence before making decisions.

I enjoy this process.

For me, sales is not only about selling products.

It is about understanding customer challenges, connecting solutions with their needs, and helping projects move forward.

This is the direction I hope to continue developing in.

---

## **Finding a Sustainable Way to Create Value**

Another important lesson from my previous experiences is that the working environment matters.

Different stages of my career have shown me that I perform better in environments where I have:

· a certain level of autonomy;

· space to think and plan;

· opportunities to take ownership;

· long-term projects that require continuous improvement.

To me, autonomy does not mean less responsibility.

It means having the ability to make decisions, organize my work, and take ownership while being accountable for results.

I have experienced different working styles throughout my career.

Some environments helped me grow quickly through pressure and challenges.

Others allowed me to develop through independent thinking and long-term accumulation.

I believe a sustainable career requires both commitment and a working approach that allows continuous growth.

The right environment should challenge me, improve my capabilities, and allow me to create value over the long term.

---

## **Redefining Career Accumulation**

Earlier in my career, I focused more on external indicators:

The company I worked for.

The position I held.

The products I represented.

Over time, I have started to think differently about professional growth.

The most valuable things are the abilities that remain with me regardless of where I work:

· understanding customers;

· communicating effectively;

· managing projects;

· solving problems;

· making better decisions.

Companies and industries may change.

But these capabilities can continue to accumulate throughout a career.

This is the kind of long-term professional value I want to build.

---

## **Becoming More Proactive in Creating Opportunities**

At the same time, I have also become more aware of the areas where I need to continue improving.

Professional growth is not only about becoming better at existing skills.

It also requires actively creating new opportunities.

In the future, I want to become better at:

· expanding my professional network;

· exploring new possibilities;

· communicating with a wider range of people;

· making decisions with more confidence.

Growth does not only come from improving what we already know.

It also comes from challenging ourselves beyond familiar ways of working.

---

## **Moving Forward With More Clarity**

Looking back, my career journey has included unexpected turns.

I did not follow a perfectly planned path.

Some choices worked well.

Some choices helped me understand what was not suitable for me.

But every experience contributed something valuable.

Today, I no longer expect myself to find a perfect career path.

Instead, I want to continue building a career that connects:

· my professional abilities;

· my personal strengths;

· my long-term career goals.

I do not know exactly where this path will lead me.

But compared with the past, I have a clearer understanding of the direction I want to move toward.

The next stage is not about starting over.

It is about continuing to build on everything I have learned so far.
