# **When Explaining Became a Way to Control and Carry Too Much**

For a long time, I thought my habit of explaining came from a desire to communicate clearly.

I believed that if I could explain my thoughts completely, organize my reasons carefully, and provide enough context, people would understand me better.

But over time, I started to realize that my need to explain was not only about communication.

There were deeper reasons behind it.

---

## **The Hidden Reasons Behind My Explanations**

One reason was my desire to control uncertainty.

When something happened differently from what I expected, I often felt the need to clarify everything.

I wanted to make sure there were no misunderstandings.

I wanted every detail to have a reasonable explanation.

I wanted the situation to return to a state where everything was clear and predictable.

Deep down, I was uncomfortable with leaving things unresolved.

An incomplete explanation felt like an unfinished task.

A misunderstanding felt like something I needed to fix.

---

But there was another reason that I only gradually became aware of.

I was not only trying to make myself understood.

I was also trying to take care of other people's emotions.

When I sensed that someone might feel uncertain, worried, or uncomfortable, my instinct was to explain more.

I wanted to remove their doubts.

I wanted to make them feel reassured.

I wanted them to understand my intentions and stop feeling uneasy.

Sometimes, there was not even a real misunderstanding.

Sometimes, the other person simply had their own feelings, concerns, or perspectives.

But I often treated their uncertainty as something I needed to solve.

---

## **The Cost of Carrying Too Much**

The problem was that this process consumed a lot of my energy.

While trying to make others feel better, I often ignored my own exhaustion.

I could spend a long time organizing my thoughts, rewriting messages, adding more explanations, and imagining how the other person might interpret my words.

I thought I was creating better communication.

But sometimes, I was only creating a bigger emotional loop.

The more I explained, the more details appeared.

The more details appeared, the more things needed to be explained.

Eventually, I found myself trapped inside my own explanations.

I was trying so hard to prevent misunderstanding that I became overwhelmed by the process itself.

---

## **Learning Where My Responsibility Ends**

Over the past year, I started to notice this pattern more clearly.

I began to ask myself:

"Is this explanation truly necessary?"

"Am I communicating because something needs to be understood, or am I trying to remove all uncertainty?"

"Am I helping the situation, or am I simply trying to make everyone feel completely comfortable?"

These questions changed the way I see communication.

I realized that clarity is valuable, but unlimited explanation is not always helpful.

Understanding someone is a two-way process.

I can express myself honestly and clearly, but I cannot control how another person interprets everything.

I can care about someone's feelings, but I do not have to carry all of their emotions.

---

## **Learning to Stop Earlier**

I am still learning this.

I have not completely changed this habit.

My first instinct is still sometimes to explain more.

When I feel tension or uncertainty, the old pattern still appears.

But now I can recognize it.

I can pause.

I can tell myself:

"Maybe this is enough."

"Maybe I don't need to solve everything right now."

"Maybe it is okay if someone does not fully understand me."

Learning to stop explaining is not about becoming indifferent.

It is about giving myself permission to rest.

It is about understanding that relationships do not become healthier because one person constantly tries harder.

Sometimes, the healthiest thing I can do is communicate honestly, accept the uncertainty, and allow both sides to carry their own emotions.

---

## **A Different Way of Relating**

Looking back, I realize that many of my old habits came from the same desire:

I wanted things to be safe.

I wanted my environment to be organized.

I wanted relationships to be stable.

I wanted people around me to feel okay.

These intentions were not wrong.

But I was using too much of my own energy to achieve them.

Growing up, I thought responsibility meant taking care of everything.

Now I am learning a different definition:

Responsibility means knowing what belongs to me — and what does not.

I can explain.

I can care.

I can try to understand.

But I do not need to carry everything.
