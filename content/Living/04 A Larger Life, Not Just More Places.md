For a long time, everyday life and exploring the world seemed like two separate things to me.

## **Eight Years in Baishilong, Shenzhen**

I lived in Baishilong, Shenzhen, for eight years. I didn't have a full two-day weekend every week. Between work and everyday household chores, such as grocery shopping, cooking, cleaning, and washing dishes, there wasn't always much time or energy left to explore the city.

That doesn't mean I never travelled or enjoyed outdoor activities. I had been to quite a few well-known hiking routes, including Dongchong-Xichong in Shenzhen, Dà Nán Shan in Huizhou, Luofu Mountain, the MacLehose Trail in Hong Kong, and Po Pin Chau.

I even found some of these hikes not challenging enough, so I signed up for the Three Shui Line, a much more demanding challenge often described as the ultimate graduation hike for outdoor enthusiasts in Shenzhen.

Unlike a regular hike, it was a timed challenge, with different medals awarded depending on how quickly participants completed the route. We started at 10 a.m. and kept moving, often jogging rather than walking, until 6 p.m., crossing more than 30 mountain peaks along the way.

I went to Dapeng and Jiao Diaosha Beach in Shenzhen, visited Double Moon Bay in Huizhou to enjoy the sea, went shopping and watched the night views in Hong Kong, visited Disneyland, and travelled to Guangzhou to try its local food.

Perhaps the difference was that my everyday life itself remained quite fixed. Back then, I felt that I needed a special landscape or a particularly memorable outing to really feel that I was experiencing life. Everyday life and these special experiences seemed to exist as two separate things, almost as if there were nothing in between.

## **A Few Trips Over the Past Year**

### **Late December 2025 | Quanzhou and Zhangzhou**

Before the New Year holiday, I took a few days off and travelled to Quanzhou and Zhangzhou, avoiding the main holiday rush.

The smell of ginger duck seemed to be everywhere in Quanzhou. For my first meal in Quanzhou, I went straight to a well-known restaurant to try its dry-style ginger duck.

It was rich, aromatic, and absolutely delicious. I enjoyed every bite and ate with great enthusiasm. My jaw, however, paid the price over the next few days. All that chewing had left it exhausted!

### **Before Chinese New Year 2026 | Ganzhou**

Before returning home for Chinese New Year, I took another few days off and visited Ganzhou.

Before this trip, I had never realised that Ganzhou, a city so close to Lingnan, had such deep historical ties to the Song Dynasty, including its ancient city walls.

I also enjoyed the local food. Even the small, ordinary restaurants I walked into served surprisingly good stir-fried dishes.

During those three days, I felt as though I had blended into the local rhythm of life. I hardly saw any other tourists.

### **Late May 2026 | Xinjiang**

In late May, I travelled to Xinjiang with a close friend. We first met up in Lanzhou before flying to Urumqi.

After arriving in Lanzhou, I went to a Lanzhou noodle restaurant for lunch. We had agreed on a meeting point, and since I arrived early, I still had plenty of time before we were due to meet. So I sat outside the restaurant, scrolling through my phone.

To my surprise, just as I was looking down at my phone, I suddenly saw my friend walking towards me.

I was so happy to see her.

Originally, she had planned to take a taxi to drop off her luggage before coming to meet me. But when she realised I was nearby, she asked the driver to stop and simply walked over with her luggage.

After meeting up, we decided to spend the afternoon by the Yellow River. We found a small tea stall along the riverbank, ordered two cups of tea, and sat there talking.

We stayed until evening and were reluctant to leave. Before heading to the airport, we made a special trip to find a local malatang shop. Malatang had become hugely popular a couple of years earlier, and I'd wanted to try it for a long time. With our flight approaching, we hurriedly ordered some to take away and then headed to the airport.

After that, we spent seven or eight days travelling the Ili loop in Xinjiang.

We joined a small tour group of six, travelling with a driver along the route. The schedule was relatively flexible, and we could talk to the driver if we wanted to spend more time somewhere or head back earlier to rest. Although the itinerary wasn't particularly rushed, covering the vast Ili loop was still quite tiring.

I had set aside about three days in Urumqi in total, before and after the road trip, as I wasn't sure how tiring the journey would be. After returning from the Ili loop, I had two days to explore Urumqi on my own.

I tried the local pilaf and Xinjiang-style fried rice noodles.

Those last two days were particularly enjoyable. I went out for meals alone, took buses and taxis around the city, wandered through different neighbourhoods, looked for food I wanted to try, and visited places I hadn't had time to see earlier.

I've even started looking forward to visiting Xinjiang again on my own next year.

### **Late September 2026 | Jingzhou**

With only three working days between the Mid-Autumn Festival and National Day holidays, I took a few days off and returned to Hubei early. During the break, I went to Jingzhou to visit a friend.

She had to work during the day, so I explored Jingzhou on my own and waited until she finished work to have dinner and catch up.

On the first day, I tried a well-known local malatang chain. Unlike the usual style, where you choose all your ingredients at once and have them cooked together before being served in a bowl, Jingzhou malatang is served around a stove. Everyone sits around it, ordering ingredients as they go, with the food being cooked and eaten along the way.

After my friend finished work in the evening, we went out for a lovely dinner and talked for a long time. We didn't part until after 10 p.m., and neither of us was particularly eager to leave.

The next day, I visited Shashi, an old part of Jingzhou.

Half a year earlier, I had come across a Xiaohongshu post about actress Yuan Quan returning to Jingzhou for a photo shoot. The cover photo featured a local beef noodle shop. I ended up saving the shop, and the more I read about it, the more I wanted to visit Jingzhou and try its beef noodles.

This time, I finally made it there.

At lunchtime, I also visited another small malatang shop run by a husband and wife. It served malatang in the same way, but had a much livelier and more homely atmosphere.

Over those two days, getting around the city by taxi and visiting different places gave me a first impression of Jingzhou and its everyday streets.

## **2026: Exploring Different Places**

On October 3, after arriving at Dongguan East Railway Station, I spontaneously decided to spend the day exploring Changping instead of heading straight back.

I first found a Chaoshan-style rice noodle soup shop for breakfast, then went to a nearby shopping mall. In the afternoon, I looked up some popular local restaurants online and decided to try one.

On October 5, I made a special trip to Wanjiang in Dongguan to watch The Odyssey at the only IMAX GT screen in South China. I had originally planned to head straight back after the movie, but decided to visit the nearby old town of Guancheng instead, where I tried some local food.

On October 10, I found a café online and made a special trip to Huaide in Bao'an, Shenzhen. It was a Grid Café filled with natural light and lush greenery.

In the evening, I went to a nearby old street to look for something to eat.

When I lived in Shenzhen, I mostly got around by metro. I would enter one station and emerge from another near my destination, with much of the journey taking place underground. The streets and neighbourhoods in between were often outside my view.

Now in Dongguan, I more often take taxis for everyday trips around the area where I live and work. Even on familiar routes, I enjoy looking out at the buildings, shops, roads, and factory areas along the way. I find myself paying attention to these everyday street scenes, even when I'm simply travelling somewhere I've been many times before.

## **Letting Life Continue to Unfold**

Exploring everyday life has also become part of my routine.

Taking walks along the river, wandering through nearby streets and looking at small shops and street stalls, making special trips to try restaurants I've discovered on Xiaohongshu or Dianping, exploring new cafés or returning to familiar favourites, and treating myself to a milk tea on Friday afternoons — these ordinary little things have gradually become part of my daily life.

I hope work and life can continue alongside each other in this way, with a steady daily routine and enough time to explore the world beyond my immediate surroundings.

**Life doesn't have to wait for a special trip to begin.** There are plenty of ordinary days worth experiencing along the way to the next journey.
