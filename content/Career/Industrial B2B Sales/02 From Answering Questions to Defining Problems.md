# **From Answering Questions to Defining Problems**

## **How I Learned to Approach Technical Issues in Industrial B2B Sales**

When I started working in industrial B2B sales, I believed that one of the most important responsibilities of a salesperson was to provide answers.

When customers had questions, my typical approach was:

Understand the issue → Find the right internal person → Collect information → Reply to the customer.

For routine inquiries, this approach works well.

However, after several years working with technical products and overseas customers, I gradually realized that complex problems require a different mindset.

The most important step is not always finding the answer.

Sometimes, it is defining the problem first.

---

## **A Technical Issue That Changed My Way of Thinking**

I once worked with an overseas research customer who reported abnormal test results after using our equipment.

The customer initially believed that there might be a hardware issue because some units showed different behavior compared with others.

At first glance, it looked like a product problem.

However, instead of immediately treating it as a hardware failure or forwarding the issue directly to the technical team, I started by reviewing the situation from a broader perspective:

· What exactly happened?

· Under what conditions did the issue occur?

· Did the testing requirements match the product capability?

· What information was still missing?

Through this process, I realized that the initial assumption was only one possibility.

Before solving a problem, we need to understand what problem we are actually solving.

---

## **The Difference Between Symptoms and Root Causes**

In industrial environments, customers usually describe problems based on their observations.

They may say:

"The equipment does not work."

"The result is not as expected."

"The performance does not meet the requirement."

These descriptions are important, but they often represent symptoms rather than the complete picture.

A technical issue may come from different factors:

· product specifications;

· application conditions;

· testing methods;

· customer expectations;

· incomplete information.

A sales engineer's responsibility is not only to listen and respond.

It is also to help identify which factors are truly influencing the situation.

---

## **From Responding to Guiding the Process**

This experience changed the way I handle technical discussions.

Previously, my focus was:

How can I give the customer an answer as quickly as possible?

Later, my focus became:

How can I help both the customer and internal teams understand the real problem?

The process I gradually developed is:

**Customer feedback**

↓

**Clarify the situation**

↓

**Check requirements and product boundaries**

↓

**Collect necessary information**

↓

**Define the problem scope**

↓

**Decide the next action**

This approach helps avoid jumping to conclusions and ensures that technical resources are focused on meaningful analysis.

---

## **The Role of a Sales Engineer in Industrial B2B**

Industrial B2B sales is different from simply selling a product.

Customers understand their applications and goals.

Engineers understand product design and technical limitations.

A sales engineer works between these two perspectives.

The value of a sales engineer is not just passing information between customers and engineers.

It is translating customer needs into clear requirements and helping both sides reach a shared understanding.

---

## **What I Learned**

Looking back, this was an important shift in my professional growth.

Earlier in my career, I focused more on execution:

· responding to customers;

· coordinating internally;

· completing assigned tasks.

With more experience, I gradually developed stronger judgment.

I learned that handling complex industrial problems is not about providing immediate answers.

It is about asking better questions, defining the real problem, and creating a clear path toward a solution.

This shift — from answering questions to defining problems — has become one of the most valuable skills I have developed through industrial B2B sales.
